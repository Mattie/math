# Four ways into the paper

These explainers accompany Ryan Matthew Casper's *The irrationality exponent of real and imaginary quadratic periods is 2*. Each can be read on its own.

| Start here | Assumed background |
|---|---|
| [High school](ELIHS.md) | Fractions, powers, and some curiosity about irrational numbers |
| [Bachelor's](ELIBS.md) | Calculus, complex numbers, and introductory proof writing |
| [Master's](ELIMS.md) | Real analysis and some algebra or number theory |
| [PhD](ELIPHD.md) | Diophantine approximation and comfort with determinant arguments |

All four explain the three families, the meaning of exponent two, and the arithmetic contradiction behind the proof. The decimal examples are illustrations; computing more digits cannot prove the theorem.
